Debenhams Group

One scaled design system serving 21 brands. Feature delivery cut from months to days, and conversion wins of up to +11.7%.

Role
UX Designer, reporting to the CTO
Timeline
August 2023 to present
Team
2 designers supporting 50+ developers
Scope
A multi-brand design system for 21 brands across app, mobile web and desktop, plus CRO, accessibility and new features

The Challenge

After the acquisition, the business ran like a startup.

The website had been built quickly by a single front-end developer and the app from a reference template. There was no design structure, no shared components and no rigour: teams were simply shipping.

Then the brief grew. Our Debenhams team moved to report directly to the CTO, with a mandate to take the system we'd built for Debenhams across the whole portfolio, from boohoo and PrettyLittleThing to Karen Millen and The Brand Room. And the business wanted to ship fast, which meant there was little appetite for up-front user testing.

So the real challenge was building rigour into speed: one system every brand could ship from quickly, without losing what makes each brand distinct.

Working with the Business

I run a bi-weekly alignment forum with the e-commerce and creative leads from every brand, covering design reviews, feedback and sign-off, plus a monthly session for longer-term ideas. It's how 21 brands' priorities become one roadmap.

Starting from the Brand

I started with the creative team. We worked through the brand guidelines, set up the core foundations of the design system, then worked component by component through the website. All of this happened while delivering a steady stream of new features, so the system had to be built in flight.

Making Clickable Mean One Thing

Mapping the estate exposed a lot of inconsistency in how calls to action looked and how colour signalled what was clickable.

We wanted to move away from underlined hyperlinks and make colour do that job reliably.

We mapped each brand's primary colour one to one, and derived a CTA colour from it. That CTA colour, and its shades, became the single signal for anything you could tap or click, on every brand.

There was pushback from parts of the business that didn't want everything clickable to carry colour. To settle it with evidence rather than opinion, we surveyed around 30 employees from outside digital and technology, to avoid internal bias. The results validated the approach, and the colour alignment rolled out across the brands.

One System, Every Brand

The system is built code-first, with Figma downstream:

  • Two layers of tokens. Primitives are named for what they are; semantic tokens are named for their role. Components only ever use semantic tokens.
  • One switch per brand. A single brand attribute on the page resolves every token to that brand's colours, fonts, weights, corner radius and text case. All 21 brands are modes of the same system, with no per-brand stylesheets and no forked components.
  • Brand differences as configuration. Square corners, for example, are a brand option rather than a separate component: PrettyLittleThing and The Brand Room switch them on with one token.
  • Accessibility as the floor. Every component targets WCAG 2.1 AA, with 4.5:1 text contrast, 44px touch targets, visible focus states and reduced-motion support built into the tokens. Status is never shown by colour alone.
  • A shared library. 18 component families, including buttons, product cards, PLP, PDP, bag and checkout kits, plus a 136-icon library, all rendered live in every brand.
  • Aligned with engineering. I mapped the system component by component against the front-end team's Storybook, 79 components and 178 stories, so design and build converge on one target.
  • Master prototypes. Each core page has a canonical version built from system components. New briefs branch from it, explore options, and merge the chosen route back, so nothing gets rebuilt from memory.

Test and Learn

With little room for pre-launch research, we made live performance the research.

Rather than one big release, we put variants live with clear hypotheses, measured them, kept the winner and kept improving. There's no start or end, just continuous improvement.

Over three years, that's meant 100+ A/B tests across the group's brands, measured on conversion, average order value, revenue per user and checkout completion. Wins include:

  • +11.7%Shop By Fit, Karen Millen
  • +2.3%AI-generated review summaries, plus +1.5% revenue per user
  • +2.2%Bag product recommendations, Debenhams
  • +1.8%Checkout address CTA redesign
  • 4.1%Click and collect conversion, up from 3.5%

Outcome

Accessibility audits and fixes helped the business avoid payouts from attempted US accessibility litigation.
